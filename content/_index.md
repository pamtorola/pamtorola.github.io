I study how micro-level forces inside farms and households — the need to feed one's family, land inheritance norms, and how women divide their time between home and market work — shape productivity and structural transformation.

My [job market paper](/jmp) studies the transition from subsistence to market-oriented farming. I show how caloric needs and fixed selling costs distort crop choices, how development induces farms to enter output markets and specialize, and how this transition contributed to agricultural productivity growth in India between 1971 and 2006.

Before my PhD, I was a predoctoral fellow to Professor William Nordhaus at Yale (2019–2021) and a research assistant at the Medical Practice Evaluation Center (Harvard / Massachusetts General Hospital), a health policy research group that builds simulation models to evaluate clinical and public health decisions. I hold an M.Phil. and M.A. in Economics from Yale (2024) and a B.S. in Ecology and Evolutionary Biology from Yale (2018). My graduate work is supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship and was supported by a Sylff Fellowship at the Yale Economic Growth Center.
